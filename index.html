What an AI engineer actually does in 2026

Not training models. Not research. Not the maths of neural networks. We analysed 50 UK AI engineer job adverts and found that more than three quarters of the role is building products on top of models that already exist: connecting a model to a company's documents, giving it tools to take actions, testing that it behaves, and shipping it so real people can use it.

That is a software job with an AI specialism, and it is learnable by a determined beginner. This programme teaches exactly that job, using the words employers use in their own adverts, so that by the end you describe your work the way the job description describes the role.

How the programme is built

The same deliberate structure that has taken our data analysis students from beginner to hired. Nothing is introduced before you have the context to understand why it matters.

1

Theory first, told as a story

Before any tool appears, you learn what AI is, how a language model answers a question, why it sometimes makes things up, and what the job really involves. Video lectures, notes to read alongside, and a quiz after each one.

2

The whole job with no code

In Stage 1 you build a complete AI assistant with no code at all: give it documents to answer from, actions it can take, test it, fix it, publish it. You will have done retrieval, tool use, evaluation and deployment before you have written a line of Python.

3

Then code, one build at a time

Python written for building services, then calling a model, then retrieval, then agents, then evaluation, then deployment. Stages 3 to 8 rebuild and extend the same application, so you finish with one real system rather than ten disconnected exercises.

Ten stages, one portfolio

Every stage ends with a build that goes into your public GitHub portfolio. The capstone is the credential: a live application, an evaluation report, and a ten-minute presentation to a business audience.

What we promise, and what we don't

We promise the capability and the portfolio: you will be able to build, evaluate and deploy a real AI application, and you will have one live to show. We do not promise a job title or a salary. The junior AI engineer market in the UK is small and often asks for a degree, and most graduates' first role is a junior developer or automation role with an AI component, typically in the low-to-mid thirty-thousands, from which the AI engineer title follows within two or three years.

Anyone promising a direct path from beginner to a £60,000 AI engineer role is not describing this market. We would rather you knew that before you enrolled than after.
