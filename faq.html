About you

Do I need a technical background or coding experience?

No. The programme starts with theory told as a story and then a complete no-code workflow before any Python appears. It is designed for people changing careers from non-technical jobs and for graduates of unrelated subjects. If you can use a computer confidently and follow instructions carefully, you can start.

Do I need a degree?

Not to join. Many junior AI engineer adverts do ask for one, and we say so plainly on the career page. The programme's answer is a portfolio strong enough to be considered without one: a deployed, evaluated application that most graduates cannot show.

Is this the same as a data analysis or data science course?

No. Data analysis is about finding answers in data; AI engineering is about building software that uses AI models. There is very little overlap in tools beyond Python. If you have done our Data Analysis Career Programme you will recognise the teaching style, but the content is new.

I already write code. Is this too basic for me?

Stages 0 to 2 will be revision, and you can move through them quickly. Stages 3 to 8, which cover LLM APIs, context engineering, retrieval, agents, evaluation and deployment, are the substance of the programme and are not covered properly by most developer courses either.

About the programme

How is it taught?

Recorded video lectures, notes and documents to read alongside them, and a quiz after each chapter to check it has landed. Every stage then has a guided build with a defined finish line. Everything is online and self-paced.

Why start with a no-code tool instead of Python?

Because understanding the shape of the job is more important than syntax. In Stage 1 you build a full AI assistant without code and are told, as you go, which later stage each button corresponds to. When Python arrives, you already know what you are building and why; you are just learning to build it by hand.

Will I learn to train my own AI models?

No, and that is deliberate. Junior AI engineer roles are about building on existing models, not training them from scratch. You learn to understand models well enough to choose, use, constrain and evaluate them, which is what the job actually involves. Fine-tuning is covered in a separate advanced programme.

Which AI models and tools does it use?

The major model providers, used through their own SDKs first so you understand what a framework is doing before you use one. Copilot Studio for the no-code stage. Python and FastAPI for services. One cloud for deployment. The programme teaches the patterns, which outlast any particular product, and we refresh the specific tools before each cohort.

How long does it take?

It depends on the hours you can give it. Self-paced, most people should expect several months of consistent part-time study. We will publish clearer guidance with the enrolment details once the full programme has been through its first learners.

What does it cost?

Pricing will be announced when enrolment opens. People on the waitlist will be told first and will be offered early-cohort pricing.

About getting a job

Do you guarantee a job?

No. Anyone who guarantees a job in this market is not being honest with you. We teach the capability, you build the portfolio, and Stage 10 gives you a realistic search plan: which titles to look for, how to spot paid courses disguised as vacancies, and how to write a CV in the vocabulary employers use.

What will my first role realistically be?

Most likely a Junior AI Developer, an AI Automation Engineer, a graduate technology role with an AI focus, or a developer or automation role with an AI component. Pay for these roles is typically in the low-to-mid thirty-thousands, higher in London. The AI engineer title tends to follow within two to three years. Read the full picture.

What do I actually have at the end?

A public GitHub portfolio with a build from every stage, one production-grade application live at a public URL, an evaluation report showing you tested it properly, and a recorded ten-minute presentation of your capstone to a business audience. That is what you show an employer.
